## 2026-11-14: D2 and D3 decision: inline frames and 16-byte slots by the rules; the pairlist control is within 5%, so it goes to the lead

- Bars: docs/ledger/g3.md, "M1 decision bars", D2 (rules 1-4) and D3.
- Runs: `2026-11-14-d2-d3-runs.md` (four factorial cells and the
  `pairlist` control, base `e2207b4d`), the base's in
  `2026-11-14-x-base-runs.md`.
- D2 rule 1, at each D3 level the lowest geomean instructions over CALLS
  and SUITE:
  - at `slot16`: `inline` 66,135.8, `frameobj` 70,158.9 (+6.08%):
    `inline`;
  - at `split`: `inline` 69,938.3, `frameobj` 74,070.7 (+5.91%): `inline`.
  Neither is inside the tie band, so the allocation and RSS tie-breaks do
  not apply.
- D2 rule 2: both levels pick `inline`, so D2's verdict is `inline`, and
  D3 is decided by its own bar at `inline`.
- D3's bar, at `inline`, the lowest geomean estimated cycles over SUITE
  and CALLS: `slot16` 71,022.8, `split` 74,881.5 (+5.43%): `slot16`.
  (At `frameobj` too: split ÷ slot16 = 1.0532.)
- D2 rule 4: `pairlist`'s geomean instructions at `slot16` are 68,106.7,
  **1.0298 × the winner's**, within the 5% the rule names (≤ 1.05).
  **So the result goes to the lead before anything is adopted.**
- Verdict: by rules 1-3, D2 `inline` and D3 `slot16`, both the defaults
  already on main, so a decision to adopt them merges no code. Rule 4
  holds that adoption for the lead. Until the lead decides, main stays as
  it is, the four variant branches stay as evidence, and nothing merges
  (no control ever does).
- For the lead (from the runs entry):
  - GNU R's representation (a pairlist frame and a PROMSXP per argument)
    costs 2.98% more instructions and 5.41% more estimated cycles than
    `inline` with delayed slots. It allocates 1.26 to 5 times the
    objects per iteration (`s3` 34 against 27, `named` 10 against 2,
    `fib` 45,984 against 20,901), so §13's zero allocations per call is
    out of its reach. It is faster on `s3` (−7.7%) and `superassign`
    (−1.8%), and slower on `named` (+12.0%) and `plain` (+6.3%).
  - Slot frames on their own do not explain the gap. Over D4's rows
    (`plain`, `defaults`, `named`, `fib`), PROMSXP arguments on slot frames
    (D4's control) are 10.07% above the slots, while the pairlist frame
    with the same arguments is 5.87% above (2.98% over all eight rows). On the base, part of the frame cost is
    avoidable: `Cx::env_has_no_special_symbols` builds and sorts a `Vec`
    of the frame's bindings on every closure call (about 160 instructions
    with its `malloc`/`free` on `plain`), the slot force path costs 316
    instructions against a promise force's 199, and a slot lookup in a
    two-slot frame costs more than a walk of two cells. These are P1's
    to remove, and each makes the margin over `pairlist` larger.
  - A suggested reading of rule 4 for the lead: the control is within 5%
    of an implementation that has not been tuned yet (P1 has not run). The
    question is whether to adopt `inline` now on that evidence, or to wait
    for P1's first steps and run the same comparison again.
- Deleted once the lead decides: `g3/x1-d2-frameobj`, `g3/x1-d3-split`,
  `g3/x1-d2-frameobj-d3-split`, `g3/x1-d2-pairlist`.
- **Lead decision (Max, 2026-11-14): D2 `inline` (typed slots with the
  overflow area) and D3 `slot16` (16-byte value-stack slots) adopted.**
  Rule 4's report stands on the record: the `pairlist` control at 1.0298 ×
  `inline`'s geomean instructions. P1 re-measures `inline` against
  `pairlist`, with this bar's rows, after its call-path fixes (the per-call
  `Vec` and sort in `Cx::env_has_no_special_symbols`, the slot lookup
  cost). So `g3/x1-d2-pairlist` is kept for that run; `g3/x1-d2-frameobj`,
  `g3/x1-d3-split` and `g3/x1-d2-frameobj-d3-split` are deleted once this
  PR merges.
