## 2026-11-16: P1 step 16, a call-site plan's hit: from the call's arguments to the frame

the plan-hit path in `args::apply_with::<false>`, which round 1

- Base: main at `bac15367` (#722, #725 and #723 in). Change: branch
  `g3/p1-call-plan`, measured at `562d439c`, its merge of that main
  (`robigo-rt`). The steps below were made and measured on main
  `9dbf19d9`; #725 (S1's promise sharing) then conflicted in `args.rs`,
  and the merge keeps both.
- **What a hit cost.** A call from compiled code whose site had a plan
  for its callee still walked the closure's formals and the call into the
  matcher's stack buffers, filled a match from the plan, and built the slot
  inits from those. On main, `args.rs`'s own lines cost 701 instructions
  per plain real call (inlining off, by callgrind's per-line attribution),
  about 160 of them the buffers' pushes, reads and length tests.
- **The change.** When the plan alone binds the call (no partial match to
  warn of, no actual gobbled by `...`), `plan_inits` reads the call's
  arguments and the formals' defaults straight into the slot inits. A
  plan is made only for a site whose call has no `...` among its arguments,
  and the call at a site is a constant of the caller's code, so a hit
  makes the inits the matcher would. If the call's or the formals' count
  is not the plan's, the inits are dropped and the matcher runs, before
  anything observable.
- **The call's actuals, read later.** The lazy `promargs` filler and S3
  dispatch's `generic_args` read a call's actuals and match while its record
  lives (`FillData`). A call bound by its plan keeps a copy of the plan
  (the site's may be replaced while the call runs), its arguments' tags,
  and the mask of those that were R_MissingArg, all taken at the call: C
  can rewrite the call while it runs, and GNU R made its list at the call.
  Both readers go through `FillData::each_actual`, which serves the
  matcher's form too.
- **The first version regressed `s3`.** Its readers rebuilt the matcher's
  buffers from the call and the plan, and UseMethod reads the generic's
  actuals on every dispatch: `s3` went up 2.4%. Step 2 reads them in place.
- **Sol's finding on step 3** (`0668729a`): the readers took the tags and
  missingness from the record's call. After C removed the call's names
  while it ran (`tests/rt/capi/plan-call-mutation.R`), the method was
  matched by position, where main and GNU R match by the names the call
  had. Step 4 keeps them from the call's start.
- **The record's push and pop, inlined or not.** Step 4's few
  instructions tipped LLVM into calling rtcore's `with_record` (the call's
  record pushed, the body run, the record popped) out of line, about 100
  more per real call; step 2 had it out of line too, so part of step 3's
  gain was its inlining. Forcing it inline (`#[inline(always)]`) made
  `s3` 0.6% slower. Step 5 moves `apply`'s error paths (a `rho` that is
  not an environment, `...` misused, too many arguments, the matcher's
  errors) to cold functions, and the record is inlined again.
