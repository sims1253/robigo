## 2026-11-16: P1, E7's paste: each piece read once, the result interned with no scan; GNU R's mkChar measured beside it

The lead accepted E7's `paste` regression for M1, with the fix in P1:
`e7-paste` 125,690 instructions per iteration on robigo-rt against today's
robigo's 49,890 (`e7-match` and `e7-unique` pass). The foreman's work items:
the intern cost per result string, measured beside GNU R's `mkChar` on the
same input, and paste's per-piece work. D10 (what gets interned at all)
stays M3.

- Base: #680's branch `g3/e7-seq` at `9b3a66ca` (the E7 rows on
  robigo-rt). Change: branch `g3/p1-e7` at `2f36351e` (`robigo-heap`,
  `robigo-rtcore`, `robigo-rtbuiltins`). Binaries: `cargo build --release
  --locked -p robigo-rtapi --bin robigo-rt` (the binary moved to
  `robigo-rtapi` with A1).
- What changes:
  - `paste_all` read every argument's piece of an element twice: once
    for the encodings, once to append it. It also asked each piece's `Str`
    whether it was ASCII, a scan. Now each piece is read once, with the
    CHARSXP's `IS_ASCII` mark (`Strings::get_or_na_ascii`; a coerced
    element is ASCII). An ASCII piece is appended as it is (its
    translation is itself, and it is never `ENC_KNOWN`).
  - The result's ASCII-ness comes from its pieces and separators. Only a
    result that translated a non-ASCII piece is scanned: in a non-UTF-8
    locale, a translation can come out ASCII.
  - Before, `Cx::mk_char` scanned the result for a NUL and the heap's
    `mk_char` scanned it for ASCII. `Cx::mk_char_joined` interns the result
    with neither scan: pieces of CHARSXPs and coerced texts hold no NUL
    (`Core::mk_char_ascii`; both checks stay as debug assertions).
- Admission: `scripts/rt-ci.py` passes at `737908dc` (the change and this
  entry; exit 0): every step, conformance 227/232 (the base's 5
  unratcheted failures). `scripts/lint.sh` passes.
- Commands: `scripts/idiom-vs-r.py --rows benchmarks/rt/calls-rows.toml
  --filter '^e7-' --robigo BIN --no-r --cycles --json ...`, twice per binary.
  Per result string: callgrind of `paste(q, i)` loops at 200 and 400
  iterations (100 strings each, as the row), the difference over the 20,000
  extra strings. Each function's self and inclusive cost comes from
  `callgrind_annotate --auto=no` (`data/.../cgdiff-any.py`). For GNU R
  4.6.2 that is `bin/exec/R --vanilla --no-echo -f` with
  `R_DEFAULT_PACKAGES=NULL`; for robigo-rt, `ROBIGO_COMPILE=1`.
- Raw data: `data/2026-11-16-p1e7-2f36351e/`: the rows' JSON per binary
  and run, and the per-string tables of both binaries and of GNU R.

The intern was already below GNU R's: 383.9 against `mkCharLenCE`'s
550.6 for the same strings. Most of the difference is GNU R's
`allocCharsxp`. robigo-rt's paste costs more than GNU R's `do_paste`
per string itself, but much less in total.

E7 after this step: `e7-paste` 95,955 instructions per iteration, 1.92x
today's robigo's 49,890 (the bar is today's robigo). What is left:

- **The intern.** Today's robigo does not intern `paste`'s results at
  all; robigo-rt interns every string, as GNU R does (D10, what gets
  interned, is M3). That is the hash, the probe, the CHARSXP's allocation
  (106.6 measured) and the insert, for 100 new strings per iteration.
  An estimate, not a measurement: the base's 383.9, less the two scans
  this step removed, puts it near 300 a string, about 30,000 of the 46,065
  instructions per iteration the row misses.
- **Paste's glue**, in `paste_all`'s 554.5 self beside the inlined intern
  (the split is not measured): the piece list, the buffer, the marks, and
  the store into the result. Then the copies (`memcpy` and the like, about
  90 measured), and the call itself amortized over the 100 strings.
