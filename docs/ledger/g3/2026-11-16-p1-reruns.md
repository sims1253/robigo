### Allocations per iteration

- **D4.** With inlining off, the control allocates per call a PROMSXP
  per argument and per defaulted formal, and argument-list cells:
  `plain` 5 objects (328 bytes) against the slots' 2.001 (184), `fib`
  33,442 against 16,737.35. The slots allocate no
  PROMSXP on any row. They allocate more REALSXP than the control on
  three rows: `fib` 8,376.35 against 8,360 (16.35 more, one per about 511
  calls), `plain` and `defaults` 1.001 against 1. Those are the count
  step 2 found (`2026-11-16-p1-forced.md`): early values set aside
  because a count was due to poll, whose expressions are then evaluated
  and their values boxed. With inlining on, the same 16.35 show on `fib`
  (167.3 against 150.95).
- **D2.** The pairlist control allocates a binding cell per variable on
  top of D4's promises: `fib` 758.75 objects per iteration with inlining
  on, against 319.25.
- **D5.** Recycling removes every frame of a recycled call. With inlining
  off, `named` and `env-fn` go from 1 object per iteration (the frame) to
  0, `plain` and `defaults` from 2.001 to 1.001 (a boxed value remains),
  `fib` from 16,737.35 to 8,376.35 and `binarytrees` from 2,554 to 1,532.
  With inlining on, the calls left are the real ones: `named` and
  `env-fn` 0.001 to 0, `superassign` 4 to 3, `fib` 319.25 to 167.3,
  `binarytrees` 2,554 to 1,532.

### The bars

**D4** (`slots` is adopted if (1) its geomean instructions over `plain`,
`defaults`, `named` and `fib` are at least 10% below the control's, and
(2) argument binding allocates nothing on those rows):
- (1) holds with inlining off: slots ÷ control = 0.8075 (control ÷ slots
  1.2384), against 0.9085 at the first run. It fails with inlining on:
  0.9972, because those rows' calls run inline and bind no arguments.
- (2) fails by the letter on `fib`, `plain` and `defaults`, in either
  mode: no PROMSXP, but more REALSXP than the control, by 16.35 per `fib`
  iteration (0.2%) and 0.001 per `plain` and `defaults` iteration.
- `s3` is 3.0% faster with the control (967 instructions), as at the first
  run (3.1%): `UseMethod` rebuilds the generic's arguments from the slot
  frame.
- **Verdict: to the lead, as the bar's failed premise says.** The lead
  adopted `slots` at the first run. The re-run asked whether P1's work
  moved the instruction margin. With real calls, it did: from 9.15% to
  19.25% below the control. Recommendation: keep `slots`, record this
  run, and delete `g3/x2-d4-promsxp` and `g3/p1-rerun-d4`, as the lead's
  decision planned. The allocation excess comes from step 2's early
  values, not from binding, and removing it is P1 work on early values.

**D2** (rule 4: if `pairlist`'s geomean instructions over CALLS and SUITE
are within 5% of the winner's, the result goes to the lead):
- `pairlist` ÷ `inline` = 1.1649 with inlining on and 1.2174 with it
  off. The pairlist control is 16.5% and 21.7% slower, outside the 5%, so
  rule 4 does not apply.
- **Verdict: `inline` stands.** `g3/x1-d2-pairlist` and `g3/p1-rerun-d2`
  are deleted with this entry's PR. `s3` is 6.2% faster with pairlist
  frames (2,001 instructions), the same cause as D4's, larger here
  because the method's frame is a pairlist too
  (`2026-11-16-p1-gaps.md`, `s3`).

**D5** (`recycle` is adopted if (a) its geomean instructions over PLAIN
are at least 2% below `none`'s, or `none` allocates per call on a PLAIN
row where `recycle` allocates nothing, and (b) the quarantine mode is
clean on the rt slice, and (c) `binarytrees` is not slower than `none`
beyond the tie band):
- (a), instructions: fails in both modes (1.0000 on, 1.0030 off).
- (a), allocations: with inlining off, `none` allocates a frame per call
  on `named` and `env-fn` and `recycle` allocates nothing there, so the
  clause holds. This is what the first decision expected once values
  were unboxed. With inlining on, no PLAIN row allocates per call under
  either variant (`none` 0.001 objects per iteration on `named`, one real
  call in a thousand), so the clause does not hold.
- (b) holds: both quarantine steps of the rt slice pass (admission
  above).
- (c) holds: `binarytrees` 1.0019 in instructions, inside the tie band.
- So `recycle` passes the bar with real calls and fails it on the
  default path, where inlining leaves the PLAIN rows no frames to reuse.
  The bar was written before inlining existed and does not say which
  path it means.
- **Verdict: to the lead.** Recommendation: adopt `recycle`. Every
  closure call that does not run inline (most real code, and every
  `superassign`, `s3` and `binarytrees` call) is a real call. On real
  calls, recycling lowers estimated cycles 4-6% on PLAIN and 2.5% on
  `binarytrees`, removes the frame allocation, and lowers `binarytrees`'
  peak RSS by 22% (29,460 to 23,052 KiB). It costs 0.1-0.6% in
  instructions. Until the lead decides, `g3/x2-d5-recycle` and
  `g3/p1-rerun-d5` stay.
