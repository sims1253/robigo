## 2026-11-17: D10 runs and decision: strings, interned results (X4)

The runs D10's bar governs (`2026-11-17-d10-bar.md`), and the decision its
rule gives: **`interned`, today's main.** The rule fails in both modes on two
of its four conditions. No difference it rests on is inside the address range
of `e7-match` or `e7-unique`.

### Variants

Each binary was built in its own checkout, at a clean tree, into its own
`CARGO_TARGET_DIR` (`~/.cache/cargo-target-x4-main` for `interned`,
`~/.cache/cargo-target-x1e` for `uncached`):

```sh
cargo build --release --locked -p robigo-rtapi --bin robigo-rt
```

`robigo-rt` is a `[[bin]]` of `robigo-rtapi`, and `cargo build -p robigo-rt`
does not rebuild it. Before it measures anything, the run script checks that
the two binaries differ.

The binary has no embedded commit: it has no `--version`, and `strings` finds
no SHA. Each one was therefore checked by rebuilding it after the runs, with
the same command, from its commit (`git rev-parse HEAD` checked, a clean
tree, no `RUSTC_WRAPPER`) into an empty `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`. Both rebuilds
have the sha256 of the binary that was measured. The `uncached` rebuild went
to a target path other than its original one and still matched, so the
target path is not part of the binary.

`uncached` is the bar's variant 2:
- `paste`/`paste0`, `sprintf`, `substr` and coercion to character
  (`as.character`, `string_elt`) make their results with the heap's
  `mk_char_uncached`: no hash, no probe, no insert, no CACHED bit.
- At their entry, `match`, `%in%`, `unique`, `duplicated`, `anyDuplicated`,
  `pmatch`, the radix sort's grouping and C's `STRING_ELT` intern each
  uncached element in place. The heap's `intern_char` puts the CHARSXP
  itself into the cache, or returns its cached twin, which then replaces it
  through the barriered store. `factor` reaches the cache through `match`.
  The base subset does not bind `levels<-.factor` yet, so its path is not
  covered.
- Temporaries that a builtin hashes without storing them are keyed by an
  interned owned copy (`StrKeys`).
- Scalar `==` in the VM and the inliner compares bytes when a string is not
  cached, and so do the "same CHARSXP" shortcuts before `Scollate`
  (`util::same_string`). No R code reaches that last change today:
  robigo-rt has no `Encoding<-` or `setEncoding`, so two strings with the
  same bytes and different encodings cannot be made.

### Why the rows move

The profile is callgrind on each binary, with the row's setup and 2,000
iterations of its body, self instructions per iteration by function. It
accounts for all of the loss on `e7-match` and `e7-unique`:

| row | `StrKeys::new` | `intern_strings_in` | sum | the row's difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `e7-match` | +9,918 | +6,686 | +16,604 | +16,638 |
| `e7-unique` | +9,009 | +6,045 | +15,054 | +14,963 |

Both functions test each element's CACHED bit on every call: the in-place
interning at the builtin's entry, and `StrKeys::new`'s check for whether its
keys need an interned copy. They do this even when, as in these rows after
their first iteration, every string is cached already. About 15 instructions
per element are spent on 1,100 elements in `e7-match` and 1,000 in
`e7-unique`. The estimated cycles rise more (1.316 on `e7-match`) because
the test reads each CHARSXP's header, which the pointer hash otherwise does
not touch.

`d10-paste-match` and `d10-ascharacter` also lose to allocation:
- `interned` finds each result in the cache and allocates nothing (14 and 1
  objects per iteration).
- `uncached` allocates a new CHARSXP for every result (114 and 100,001), and
  `d10-paste-match` then hashes and probes for it at `match` as well.

`d10-ascharacter` makes no pointer-identity use, so it runs neither scan, and
its whole +3.75% comes from those allocations. `d10-paste-match` pays the
scans as well. A callgrind profile of its loop on the `uncached` binary, at
N = 2,000 against 4,000, puts `StrKeys::new` at 9,986 and
`intern_strings_in`'s own instructions at 9,983 per iteration; on
`interned`, `StrKeys::new` is 68 (`data/2026-11-17-d10-dccffc87/paste-match-profile.txt`). The profile gives a higher total than the
bar's run (323.8k against 313.1k). Taking the 19.9k off the bar's 313,080
leaves 1.054 of `interned`'s: the scans account for a little over half of the
row's loss. That subtraction is reasoned, from the measured profile.

**A substitution, computed from these numbers.** With `e7-match` and
`e7-unique` set to exactly 1.0 (their whole loss is the two scans), the
STRINGS geomean is 0.9861: 1.4% better, not the 3% the rule needs. This
substitution covers those two rows only. It is not a floor for every way of
leaving strings out of the cache:
- `d10-paste-match`, with its scans also taken off as above, would be about
  1.054, which is still over +1%;
- `d10-ascharacter`'s +3.75% is this design's allocation per result, which
  another design might avoid.

What `uncached` gains is what the bar expected: 19% fewer instructions on
`e7-paste`, 4.3% fewer on `d10-sprintf`, and 20 MB less peak RSS on 1e6
unique strings. The RSS saving is most likely the cache table's slots and its
growth; that is a judgement, not measured. What it loses on sharing shows in
`strings-dup.R`: 1e6 strings with ten distinct values take 2.8 times the
memory, because each of them is its own CHARSXP.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.9990 | 1.0075 | 1.0005 | 0.9996 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0033 | 0.9974 | 1.0009 |

`defaults` (+0.75%, +15 instructions per iteration) is over `perf-rt`'s
0.5%. The row compares strings (`label == "zz"`), and the variant changed
that path: `ops::str_eq` now tests both strings' CACHED bits before it
compares their bytes. These runs do not separate that cost from code layout,
so the cause is not established. It is moot because `interned` wins.

### For later

- **`e7-paste`, the gain left open.** `uncached` takes 0.811 of
  `interned`'s instructions on `e7-paste`, and the row is still 3.6% under
  its 2.0x ceiling. That gain is the cache's hash, probe and insert, which
  `uncached` skips when `paste` makes its results. A change to `paste` alone
  that does not touch `match` or `unique` is still open for P1 or P3: for
  example, a cheaper interning path for `paste`'s results. It must keep the
  D10 cases passing.
- **The two-row substitution.** With `e7-match` and `e7-unique` at 1.0 (no
  scan cost), the STRINGS geomean is 0.9861, which fails the 3% condition.
  Two more rows also fail the +1% condition:
  - `d10-paste-match` is about 1.054 even without its scans (reasoned, from
    the profile above);
  - `d10-ascharacter`'s +3.75% has no scan in it, so it is this design's
    allocation per result.

  An attempt that keeps strings out of the cache when they are made has to
  avoid both that allocation and the scans, not just the scans.
