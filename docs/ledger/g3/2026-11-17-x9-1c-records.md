## 2026-11-17: X9-1c, the context-record stack: R recursion stops at `expressions`, as in GNU R

The second half of X9-1's C-stack work. The first half is X9-1a
(`2026-11-17-x9-1a-cstack.md`, #792): it brought a call level to 1,844 B and
recorded GNU R's design and the budget. This is step (c), which the foreman
approved with five conditions.

### What changed

- **`robigo-rtcore/src/records.rs`, new.** The isolate owns a record stack:
  an arena of 64-slot chunks. A slot holds a `Context` (GNU R's `RCNTXT`
  layout, 528 B with its 200-byte jump buffer), a 400 B fill area and a link
  to the next slot.
  - Taking a slot is two loads, a test and a store. Releasing one is a
    single store, strictly last in, first out.
  - A chunk is made the first time the stack gets that deep.
- **`Cx::with_record`** takes the next slot instead of a `MaybeUninit`
  local. Its `Unlink` guard unlinks the record and releases the slot on
  return, on an error and on a panic.
- **`Cx::with_reserved`** reserves a slot ahead. `apply_with` reserves it
  before `prepare`, so records pushed while it matches arguments (a warning,
  an early value) take later slots. It places robigo-rt's `FillData` (the
  lazy `promargs` data) in the slot's fill area; a compile-time assert checks
  its size and alignment, so rtcore needs no robigo-rt type.
  `function_context_raw` takes the reservation by value. A `FillGuard` drops
  a matcher-made `FillData` before the slot is released.
- **Tracing is unchanged.** Records are traced through the context chain from
  the innermost, as before. The arena is not a root.

### The foreman's conditions

1. **No address arithmetic with the C stack.** Nothing in GNU R 4.6.2 or
   robigo relates a record's address to the C stack. The grep is in X9-1a's
   entry. GNU R 4.6.2 itself keeps a byte-code call's `RCNTXT` off the C
   stack, on its node stack.
2. **Bounded retention, freed.** Chunks are kept up to the high-water mark
   and freed when the isolate drops. LSan reports no leak. As a check,
   removing the freeing made LSan report 599,040 B leaked in 10 allocations.
3. **Never read before filled.** Under `debug_assertions`, a released slot's
   record and fill area are filled with `0xdd`. Chain walks and `begin`
   assert that they never meet a popped record, and a release out of order
   panics. The rtcore tests below check both.
4. **perf-rt.** See "Perf". Every row is within the gate, so nothing needed
   hiding. One variant that inlined the reservation into `vm::run` cost
   `plain` +0.7% and was dropped.
5. **Measured.** See "Depth".

### Depth (measured)

The probe is `data/…/depth.R`. The table gives the levels reached for each
`ulimit -s`, from `depth.txt`.

| binary | 2 MB | 3 MB | 4 MB | 8 MB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| main `0681a38d` (X9-1a) | 1,070 | 1,611 | 2,153 | 4,321 (C stack) |
| X9-1c | 2,093 | 3,148 | 4,205 | **4,988** (`expressions`) |

- Bytes per level, from the usage the C-stack error prints at 2 and 4 MB:
  (3,984,665 − 1,988,921) / (4,205 − 2,093) = **945 B** for X9-1c. Main's
  slope over the same stacks, using `R_CStackLimit` = 0.95 × the stack
  size, is 1,992,294 / 1,083 = 1,840 B. At 945 B, 0.95 × 8 MB would hold
  about 8,400 levels.
- At 8 MB, X9-1c stops at **4,988**: "evaluation nested too deeply:
  infinite recursion / options(expressions=)?", GNU R 4.6.2's limit and its
  message.
- gdb frames per level: `vm::run` 512 + `vm::exec` 160 + `apply_out_of_line`
  272 = 944 B, against X9-1a's 528 + 160 + 1,152.
- A calling handler that runs `f(400)` at the depth limit aborted X9-1a with
  a Rust stack overflow. It now runs.
- **`eval-depth-limit` stays blocked, but not on the C stack.**
  `options(expressions=)` is on robigo-rt's not-yet-supported list, and the
  overflow is a `simpleError`, not `expressionStackOverflowError`. Both are
  S1's S3x-b. The foreman assigned `eval-depth-limit`,
  `jit/native-closures` and `basics/nested-inline-calls` to be ratcheted
  with it.
