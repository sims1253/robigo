## 2026-11-18: X8: D12's premise failed

X8's bar (`2026-11-18-x8-bar.md`, signed at `4a9901ac9`) was never cited:
no variant was built and no run was made under it. Before building, a
check of its precise-roots condition (amendment 4) showed that D12's
premise does not hold. By the bars' protocol a failed premise goes to the
lead. This entry gives the premise, why it fails, a bound on what a remap
could save, and the rule under which a remap would be GNU R-equivalent.
The data is in `data/2026-11-18-x8/`.

### The premise and why it fails

D12 assumed that a large unshared vector being grown is held only by the
root its growth is given. Then its pages could move and the old range be
made inaccessible (the bar's amendments 2 and 4).

GNU R's complex-assignment protocol keeps other holders naming the source
during its growth. robigo-rt ports it as written (main `eec051b83`):
- `crates/robigo-rt/src/bcassign.rs:296-327`, `STARTASSIGN`: the target's
  value is read from its binding into a root (`value`), and the binding
  keeps naming it.
- `bcassign.rs:345-351`: the setter (`[<-` →
  `crates/robigo-rtbuiltins/src/subassign.rs:356` → `enlarge_vector`)
  grows it. The setter's argument slot and `enlarge_vector`'s root name it
  too.
- `bcassign.rs:352-362`, `ENDASSIGN`: only here does `define_var` store
  the result.
- `crates/robigo-rtcore/src/env/chain.rs:26-37`: that store increfs the
  old value on the assignment-pending path, then `set_car` decrefs it. It
  reads the old object's header. Frame slots (`frame::store_value`) and
  base bindings (`Loc::store`) do the same.
- The AST path (`crates/robigo-rt/src/assign.rs`) has the same shape,
  with `*tmp*` bound as well.

So at the move there are at least four holders: the binding, `value`, the
setter's argument and `enlarge_vector`'s root. Safepoints lie between the
move and `ENDASSIGN`, and the store reads the old header. Under the bar's
amendment 4 ("a call site where it cannot be shown copies"), every R-level
growth would copy. Amendment 10 would then make the `mremap` variant "not
measurable" on any machine.

**A readable tombstone header for the old range is not enough either.**
After `EnlargeVector`, GNU R runs code and raises errors that see the old
vector intact. Recorded on GNU R 4.6.2 (`data/2026-11-18-x8/oracle/`,
`.R` with its `.Rout`); robigo-rt on main gives the same output:

| case | what GNU R shows after the growth |
|---|---|
| `locked-binding`: `x[5] <- 9` on a locked binding, under `tryCatch` | the error comes at `ENDASSIGN`; `x` is still `1 2 3` |
| `warn2`: `x[4:5] <- c(7, 8, 9)` with `options(warn = 2)` | `VectorAssign`'s warning, made an error after the growth; `x` is still `1 2 3` |
| `calling-handler`: the same with `withCallingHandlers` | the handler runs R code after the growth and sees `length(x) = 3` |
| `calling-handler-list`: the same on a list | the handler sees `length(l) = 2` |
| `outer-setter`: `x$a[5] <- 9` with an R-level `$<-.cls` | the method sees `x$a` intact |

With a tombstone, each would see a length-0 husk. Interrupts, finalizers
and `*tmp*` reach the same window only through safepoints or R code in
it, so the rule below excludes them too.

### The bound: what a perfect remap could save

The binary is robigo-rt release at main `eec051b83`
(`data/2026-11-18-x8/bound/binary-sha256.txt`).

**Growth counts and bytes copied**, from robigo's `EnlargeVector` rule
(capacity ⌊newlen × 1.05⌋):

| program | growths | of them with a large source | bytes copied | at `R_EXPAND_FRAC=2` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `growdbl.R` (1e6 doubles) | 232 | 99 (155 MiB) | 156.2 MiB | 19 growths, 8.0 MiB |
| `listgrow.R` (2e5 elements) | 199 | 66 (30 MiB) | 31.2 MiB | 17 growths, 2.0 MiB |

**Instructions** (callgrind, `bound/callgrind-totals.txt`, `bound/cgdiff-*.txt`):

| program | at 1.05 | at 2.0 | removed at 2.0 | preallocated twin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `growdbl.R` | 6.000 G | 5.785 G | 215 M (3.6%) | 5.443 G |
| `listgrow.R` | 1.501 G | 1.192 G | 309 M (20.6%) | 1.138 G |

- The difference between the two factors is the copy and fill work that
  fewer, larger growths remove: an upper bound on a remap's saving in
  instructions.
- **`growdbl.R`:** grown minus preallocated is 557 M. `enlarge_vector`'s
  own cost is 333 M, of which 213 M is the copy, at 11 instructions per
  element (the `alloc_with` closure, not a block copy). The other 120 M
  is the million calls that grow in place, which a remap does not touch.
- **`listgrow.R`:** about 81 instructions per element copied, most of
  them reference-count work (`UniqueList::set_na` and the barrier's
  checks). GNU R-equivalence keeps the counts: a dead container keeps its
  children's counts (g3.md §4), which decides later duplications and so
  the address relations. A remap could remove only the memory movement.

**Faults and memory:** `bound/copybench.c` replays the realized sequence
in C (desktop, 2026-11-18). A `memcpy` with first-touch faults, against an
ideal growing `mremap`:

| | copy | ideal remap | saved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `growdbl.R`'s sequence | 23.3 ms | 5.0 ms | 18.3 ms |
| `listgrow.R`'s sequence | 4.5 ms | 1.0 ms | 3.5 ms |

**Wall time could not resolve 10% on this machine.**
- The desktop's load average was 27 to 36 throughout, from other agents'
  builds (recorded per round in `bound/wall*.json`).
- Two 11-round runs gave `growdbl.R` medians of 0.734 s and 1.009 s, a
  35% move with no code change. Grown minus preallocated came out at
  0.14 to 0.19 s for `growdbl.R` and 0.04 to 0.07 s for `listgrow.R`.
- The instruction bound stands in for wall time.

**The bound:**
- `growdbl.R`: the copy's 213 M instructions (about 21 ms, at the
  preallocated twin's rate) and the faults on the copied part (about
  15 ms) are about 36 ms. That is about 4–6% of the program at its
  fastest observed runs.
- `listgrow.R`: the memory movement alone (about 11 of the 81
  instructions per element) and its faults, about 6%.

Both are under D12's 10% on both programs. Confidence is moderate: this is
an instruction count plus a fault model, not a §13 wall time.

### The rule under which a remap would be GNU R-equivalent

This is the design of record, if growth is ever worth it. The move may
happen only after the point where no R code, error or safepoint can occur
before the binding holds the result:

1. **The permit.** `bcassign.rs` issues it at `STARTASSIGN`, on the
   bytecode path only, when all of these hold:
   - the plan has no getter steps (the target is the variable itself);
   - there is one default `[<-`/`[[<-` setter on a non-object;
   - the binding is ordinary, unlocked and not active, with no
     assignment already pending.

   `enlarge_vector` sees it through a field on the isolate. The source
   must also be large, unshared, never handed to C, and not pinned or
   immortal.
2. **Pure append only.** Under the permit every index written is at least
   the old length; anything else copies. The setter moves nothing:
   - it reserves the new range, which is the only fallible syscall, at
     the point where GNU R allocates;
   - it buffers the new elements;
   - `VectorAssign` and its warnings, handlers and errors run against
     the intact old vector.
3. **The commit at `ENDASSIGN`,** after the lock check:
   - one growing `mremap(old, old_len, new_len, MREMAP_MAYMOVE |
     MREMAP_FIXED, new)`, with a copy into the reservation as its
     fallback, which cannot fail;
   - fill the tail and write the buffered elements;
   - rewrite the header, and for lists increment each child's count (GNU
     R's copy does);
   - store the binding without the old value's decref (the source's
     count is 1 and it dies here).

   There is no safepoint until the store. The old range keeps a readable,
   length-0 header page for the stale roots (`value`, the setter's
   argument), which no R code reads after the commit. The rest is
   reserved `PROT_NONE` until the sweep that releases its VECRECs.
4. **An error before the commit** releases the reservation; nothing has
   moved, so nothing is rolled back.

**Cost:** about 4 to 6 PRs:
- the permit and the commit in the assignment protocol (`bcassign.rs`);
- a pure-append path in `VectorAssign` and list assignment;
- the heap's reserve and commit;
- a debug hook that collects at the commit;
- the five cases above as oracle cases.

All of this is for a gain bounded at about 4–6%.
